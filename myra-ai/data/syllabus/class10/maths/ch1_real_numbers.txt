Chapter 1: Real Numbers (Class 10 Mathematics - CBSE/NCERT Syllabus)

The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic states that every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur. For example, 24 can be written uniquely as 2 x 2 x 2 x 3 or 2^3 x 3. Any composite number n can be written in the form n = p1^a1 * p2^a2 * ... * pk^ak where p1 < p2 < ... < pk are prime numbers and a1, a2, ..., ak are positive integers.

Calculating HCF and LCM using Prime Factorisation:
The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two positive integers is the product of the smallest power of each common prime factor involved in the numbers.
The Least Common Multiple (LCM) of two positive integers is the product of the greatest power of each prime factor involved in the numbers.
For any two positive integers a and b, an essential relationship holds:
HCF(a, b) * LCM(a, b) = a * b.
For example, find the HCF and LCM of 6 and 20:
6 = 2^1 * 3^1
20 = 2^2 * 5^1
HCF(6, 20) = 2^1 = 2
LCM(6, 20) = 2^2 * 3^1 * 5^1 = 4 * 3 * 5 = 60
Notice that 2 * 60 = 120, and 6 * 20 = 120. The product of HCF and LCM equals the product of the numbers. However, this formula HCF * LCM = a * b holds strictly for two numbers and does NOT apply to three or more numbers.

Revisiting Irrational Numbers:
A number is called irrational if it cannot be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q is not equal to zero.
Theorem on Irrationality: Let p be a prime number. If p divides a^2, then p divides a, where a is a positive integer.
Proof that square root of 2 (√2) is irrational:
Assume to the contrary that √2 is rational.
Then there exist co-prime positive integers a and b (where b is not 0) such that √2 = a / b.
Squaring both sides gives 2 = a^2 / b^2, which implies 2 * b^2 = a^2.
Therefore, 2 divides a^2. By our theorem, 2 must also divide a.
Hence, we can write a = 2c for some integer c.
Substituting a = 2c into 2 * b^2 = a^2 gives:
2 * b^2 = (2c)^2 = 4c^2, which simplifies to b^2 = 2c^2.
This implies 2 divides b^2, and therefore 2 divides b.
Thus, both a and b have 2 as a common factor.
This contradicts the fact that a and b are co-prime (having no common factor other than 1).
This contradiction arises because of our incorrect assumption that √2 is rational.
Therefore, √2 is irrational. The same proof applies to √3 and √5.
